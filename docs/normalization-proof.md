# Normalization Proof

Generated by `python -m app.proof` from `spec/fixtures.json`. Do not edit numbers by hand; rerun the script.

## 1. The problem on this data

- 122 reviews of 40 projects by 30 judges (the duplicate `prj_41` and its 4 reviews are excluded first).
- Reviews per project: {2: 8, 3: 26, 4: 2, 5: 4} (reviews: projects). Judges are track-scoped, so no two judges see the same set.
- Reviews per judge range from 1 to 11.
- Constant rater: `jdg_07` (Iva Petrova) gave every criterion of every project the same value. `jdg_01` has a single review, which is not evidence of anything.

A raw mean rewards whichever projects happened to draw lenient judges. Per-judge z-scores fix leniency only if every judge saw a random sample of quality; with track-scoped judges they did not, so z-scoring punishes a judge who drew a strong track.

## 2. Method

Each review is first collapsed to a weighted score `y = ╬ú w_c x_c` with `╬ú w_c = 1` (the organizer's rubric weights; equal on the fixture). Then:

```text
y_jp = ╬╝ + ╬╕_p + b_j + ╬╡_jp

minimize  ╬ú (y_jp ΓêÆ ╬╝ ΓêÆ ╬╕_p ΓêÆ b_j)┬▓  +  ╬╗_judge ╬ú b_j┬▓  +  ╬╗_project ╬ú ╬╕_p┬▓
```

- `b_j` is judge j's leniency (positive = generous). `╬╕_p` is project p's quality relative to the average `╬╝`.
- This is the best linear unbiased predictor of a crossed random-effects model with `╬╗ = ╧â┬▓_noise / ╧â┬▓_effect`. Judges are compared only through projects they share, which is exactly the information an incomplete, track-scoped design contains.
- Defaults: `╬╗_judge = 2.0` (a judge's offset counts as two pseudo-reviews at zero, so one harsh review cannot be written off as a harsh judge), `╬╗_project = 1.0` (a two-review project is pulled toward the mean more than a five-review one).
- Solved by GaussΓÇôSeidel backfitting. The objective is strictly convex for positive ╬╗, so the solution is unique and the iteration converges; on the fixture it took 70 sweeps to reach 1e-10.
- Constant raters (ΓëÑ3 reviews, zero variance) are excluded by default and listed on the run. Their scores carry no ordering information; left in, they only compress their projects toward each other. Organizers can keep them with `drop_constant_raters=false`.
- Reported score: `╬╝ + ╬╕_p` on the original 1ΓÇô5 scale, with `╧â╠é/ΓêÜ(n_p + ╬╗_project)` as an approximate standard error.

## 3b. Checks on the estimate itself

**Exact solution.** Solving the normal equations directly (Gaussian elimination on all 70 unknowns, `judging_math.exact_additive`) agrees with the backfitting solver to within **3.2e-10** on every parameter. The published numbers are the unique optimum, not an early stop.

**Judge spread** (the quantity in the brief's FIG. 03). The standard deviation of the 29 judges' mean scores is **0.410** raw and **0.239** after removing each judge's offset. What remains is the real difference in the projects each judge happened to see.

**Residuals.** 88% of residuals fall within ┬▒1╧â╠é and 100% within ┬▒2╧â╠é (a normal distribution gives 68% and 95%). The largest is -1.24 (-1.7╧â╠é). No review is beyond ┬▒2.5╧â╠é, the threshold at which the portal flags a review to the organizer as a possible collusion or data-entry problem. Residuals are more concentrated than a normal would be because ╧â╠é divides by N ΓêÆ P ΓêÆ J + 1 degrees of freedom, which ignores that shrinkage spends fewer. So ╧â╠é, and every standard error built from it, errs on the wide side: conservative, which is the direction to err for prize decisions.

**Leave one judge out.** Refitting 30 times, each time without one judge: the ranking keeps Kendall ╧ä ΓëÑ **0.831** against the full fit (least robust when Diego Herrera `jdg_24` is removed, largest single-project move 17 places). The top 5 keeps at least 4 of its 5 members in every refit, so no single judge decides who wins. Mid-table projects are more fragile: a project with two or three reviews that loses one can move far, which is what the largest move above is.

**Bootstrap rank intervals.** Resampling each project's reviews with replacement 300 times and refitting gives a 90% interval for every rank (Appendix A, second column). Median width: 22 places; the top project's interval is 1ΓÇô6. With 2ΓÇô5 reviews per project, neighbouring ranks are not statistically distinguishable, which is why the results page shows the SE and why prize decisions near a boundary deserve a pairwise tie-break.

## 5. Limits we accept

- Additive leniency only: a judge who stretches the scale (uses 1 and 5 where others use 2 and 4) is modelled as noise, not as a scale factor. A multiplicative term needs more reviews per judge than the fixture has (median 3ΓÇô4).
- The standard error ignores uncertainty in the offsets, so treat adjacent ranks within one SE as ties. The portal shows SE next to every score for that reason.
- Rounding to integers and clipping at 1 and 5 are not modelled; the Monte-Carlo includes both and the method still wins.
